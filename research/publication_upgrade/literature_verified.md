## What the literature changes

The paper should not claim a new theory of noninterference, relational cost, probabilistic program equivalence, or workflow graph checking. Relational cost analysis already proves relations between the costs of two executions; constant-resource typing already joins resource use and information-flow tracking; and higher-order refinement systems combine relational cost with functional properties. Çiçek et al. (POPL 2017), Ngo et al. (IEEE S&P 2017), and Radiček et al. (POPL 2018) make those boundaries explicit. Ngo et al.'s *Bounded Expectations* also shows that stochastic programs are amenable to resource analysis. An argument that randomness makes formal resource reasoning impossible would be false. Expected cost, equality of two usage-trace laws, and a worst-case bound are different claims.

Relational reasoning through products/self-composition, as well as equivalence for probabilistic and rational weighted automata, is established prior art. Barthe, Crespo, and Kunz (2016) survey and compare product-program constructions and relational logics. Kiefer et al. (CAV 2011) treat language equivalence for probabilistic automata; their later rational-weighted-automata work (LMCS 2013) treats both expectation and distribution equivalence. Consequently, OrchLang should describe any paired graph traversal or exact law comparison as a workflow-specific application and implementation. The potentially distinctive engineering question is whether the chosen source fragment, resolved model contracts, request descriptors, and selected usage observer are faithfully connected end to end—not a new bisimulation or span algorithm.

Recent LLM workflow papers close several broad novelty claims. PPDL (ICML 2026) is a probabilistic programming language for multi-call LLM/tool flows and uncertainty propagation. ProMCP (Findings of ACL 2026) measures token and latency costs over an MCP pipeline. Agentproof (arXiv v1, March 2026) extracts workflow structures from LangGraph, CrewAI, AutoGen, and Google ADK and applies static structural and temporal checks. Its evaluation is explicitly a curated 18-workflow benchmark, not a prevalence estimate, and its scope excludes generated-text semantics. FLOWSEAL (arXiv v1, September 2026) and APPA (arXiv v2, August 2026) are recent LLM-agent confidentiality/IFC systems. They are preprints in the versions checked here, but they make “agent confidentiality is unaddressed” and “workflow security is only runtime prompting” untenable.

The narrowest defensible property is equality of a declared billable-usage observation under a specified source/graph semantics and a shared, request-sensitive abstract outcome model. That statement has real limits: request descriptors are not provider-complete wire requests; an abstract usage oracle is not a measured provider law; a per-call byte estimate is not a tokenizer or invoice guarantee; and usage-only equality says nothing about prompt confidentiality, model-output confidentiality, cache timing, network traffic, or other observers. Gu et al.'s ICML 2025 audit finds cross-user prompt-cache timing leakage at multiple providers, which makes the observer boundary material rather than cosmetic.

The paper's result can be useful without being a new theory. A concrete discriminating example for the exact usage-law checker is two secret-selected requests with different request identities but equal declared usage-trace laws. A request-equality sufficient check rejects this case, while an exact usage-observer analysis can accept it, provided all later control and usage events are modeled. Keep this as a conditional, finite model example; it does not establish that a commercial provider gives the specified law. If the final artifact does not actually exhibit this distinction in a pinned test and compare it against the structural baseline, remove the claim.

The frozen third-party corpus provides a deliberately sobering transferability result: 12 selected repositories, 4,348 tracked Python files, 59 AST-selected original LangGraph candidate files across 9 repositories, 0 admitted by the evaluated frontend, and therefore no original-source differential runs. Three selected repositories had no candidate file. This does not measure Python/LangGraph prevalence: selection is a high-star GitHub search convenience sample and admission is specific to one narrow grammar. It does show that authored projections and the separate incident-triage source case cannot support a claim of broad external-source coverage. Keep their evidence strata separate. The `16/16` source-specific stages reported elsewhere are useful implementation evidence, not independent human review or external-corpus transfer.

The strongest revision strategy is to foreground the repaired source-semantics boundary and show one meaningful case where the selected usage observer changes the result relative to the structural rule. Treat the finite-law algorithm as established mathematics applied to this domain. Report the no-admission corpus as a limitation and as an explicit frontend boundary; do not widen claims to general LangGraph or Python. Avoid “first,” “breakthrough,” “provider-secure,” and prevalence language unless later independent evidence warrants it.

For this manuscript, retain SCP as the first choice and decide between its Research Papers and Software tracks after the final claims are settled. JSS is the strongest journal alternative if the paper keeps a substantial research-method/evaluation center. JOSS should be revisited only after its explicit age, iteration, and research-use gates are met. None should be represented as having a higher acceptance probability based on this evidence.

I verified their version/date and abstract-level statements from arXiv; any detailed claim beyond those abstracts should be checked against full text before citation.
