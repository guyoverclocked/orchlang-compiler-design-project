# SCP and recent agent-workflow assurance: focused prior-art comparison

**Checked:** 14 October 2026. **Scope:** targeted, primary-source-led comparison of work published or circulated in 2025–2026 that is close to OrchLang's request-sensitive usage analysis, probabilistic trace analysis, resource bounds, workflow assurance, or information-flow claims. This is not a systematic review, exhaustive search, or priority determination. OpenAlex was used to discover and cross-check records; scientific descriptions below are based on the publisher, author, or project sources listed in the [source ledger](SOURCE_LEDGER.md). Where a publisher page or full text could not be inspected, that limit is stated.

## What OrchLang currently establishes

The project has several related but distinct artifacts. They should not be collapsed into one broad claim such as “provider usage is verified.”

| Claim/artifact | Current object and result | Defensible boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Request equivalence in the compiler | A sufficient relational check compares resolved model-contract identity, exact decoded DSL template bytes, ordered argument descriptors, and aligned references to earlier call results across secret-controlled alternatives. The shared deterministic mock oracle is keyed by contract, invocation ordinal, and that abstract request. | This is request equality **inside the DSL abstraction**. It is not equality of a provider's complete serialized request: SDK-added instructions, tool schemas, options, serialization, retries, hidden state, and wire behavior are not all modeled. The cost estimate has its own explicit per-call accounting premise. See [`FORMAL_CONTRACT.md`](../../../docs/FORMAL_CONTRACT.md). |
| Exact ordered usage-law comparison | The finite trace-law analyzer accepts a supplied finite rational outcome kernel, including complete modeled request, per-contract ordinal saturated at 33, and retained complete-event history; it decides equality of success-only ordered usage-trace laws for every finite horizon in the closed model. It emits a proof or finite distinguishing word, and a separately structured replayer reconstructs the finite system and checks the proof/witness. | This validates a declared mathematical model, not the law of an external model provider. It does not establish that supplied probabilities are correct, nor include provider failures, retry semantics, invoice rules, or whole-run infinite behavior. It is an application of established rational weighted-automaton equivalence techniques, not a new general algorithm. See [`TRACE_EQUIVALENCE.md`](../../../docs/TRACE_EQUIVALENCE.md). |
| History-dependent robust bound | One conservative checker bounds finite full-history trace TV under caller-supplied row TV balls. A separate exact checker preserves both prefix masses and computes worst-case finite trace TV for independently chosen convex hulls of rational row generators. | Row centers and radii are assumptions, not estimates or calibrated confidence sets. The result is exact for the declared rectangular contract; provider inclusion, shared hidden parameters, out-of-alphabet events, and unmodeled observer fields are outside it. Its certificate replay is independently structured but not formally verified. See [`exact_uncertain_distance/README.md`](../exact_uncertain_distance/README.md) and [`robust_usage/README.md`](../robust_usage/README.md). |
| Normalized-IR proof | Coq proves returned-state and finite-prefix refinement between source and eager-update executions of the stated normalized IR, given ordered paired nodes, `safe_after`, uniqueness/completeness, route-pairing, and initialization bridge obligations. Cycles are covered for finite prefixes. | The theorem does not prove AST extraction, source-to-IR construction, runtime/provider behavior, usage distributions, confidentiality, or termination. The source/extractor correspondence is inspectable but remains a trusted bridge, not a Coq theorem. See [`proof/RESULT.md`](../proof/RESULT.md). |
| Source-slice capsules | Two immutable public-source slices run through pinned LangGraph, with real LangChain Core in one case, under a local finite mock oracle and bounded schedule. The verifier checks hashes, schedules, projections, replay, and exact rational laws. | These are **offline capsule artifacts**, not live-provider experiments or representative workflow validation. The mock doubles replace the provider-facing factories/boundaries, so the original SDK/provider/model request path has not been exercised. Their finite supports establish no distributional or probability-radius premise. See [`workflow_capsules/REPORT.md`](../workflow_capsules/REPORT.md). |

The compiler's abstract `Q` is exact relative to DSL template bytes and ordered values. A source-derived envelope recorded for some future transport is a different layer: observing an OpenAI Responses request envelope would validate only the exercised OpenAI Responses envelope path and its captured bytes/fields. It would not show that an original Google/Gemini, Anthropic, LangChain, or other SDK call serializes equivalently, nor would it retroactively validate the two capsule workflows. Each original provider/SDK/model boundary requires a matching configuration and exact serialization path to be exercised. No such calibration is reported here.

## Claim-by-claim comparison

| OrchLang claim | Closest checked prior art | Overlap | Difference and remaining gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Secret alternatives preserve a declared ordered request signature and matched calls | Resource-aware noninterference and relational cost analysis in the existing [prior-art matrix](../../../docs/PRIOR_ART_MATRIX.md); workflow budget systems such as [typed-resources](https://github.com/hernaninverso/typed-resources) and [Costwright](https://github.com/eleata/costwright) | Relational cost and information-flow coupling are established, and current agent systems already analyze declared request/call costs. A request-sensitive key is useful engineering structure for composing an external call through both arms. | The checked LLM-agent resource systems emphasize unary pre-run ceilings and declared per-call caps. The OrchLang descriptor check instead aligns two secret alternatives under a shared oracle. Neither approach makes its request identity provider-complete. A defensible claim is this particular integration into OrchLang's restricted call semantics, not a new NI or resource-analysis principle. |
| Exact equality of ordered usage-trace laws | [PPDL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/mandel26a.html), [ProMCP](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1967/), probabilistic automata and Q-weighted automata cited in [`TRACE_EQUIVALENCE.md`](../../../docs/TRACE_EQUIVALENCE.md) | PPDL makes uncertainty in multi-call LLM/tool flows explicit; ProMCP measures token/latency flows; established automata algorithms decide rational distribution equivalence. Trace-level objects and token usage are therefore active neighboring themes. | PPDL's published aim is uncertainty propagation and inference scaling; ProMCP is empirical profiling. Neither checked publisher abstract claims exact equality of the secret-conditioned ordered usage laws for OrchLang's declared finite kernel. OrchLang's formal result is itself conditional on that kernel and request graph. Its novelty is a specialized application with proof/witness replay, not a new equivalence algorithm or evidence about a real provider. |
| Robust TV bound when the conditional usage kernel is uncertain | Work on total-variation ambiguity, imprecise Markov models, coupling, finite-trace TV, and approximate bisimulation collected in [`exact_uncertain_distance/README.md`](../exact_uncertain_distance/README.md) and [`robust_usage/OPPORTUNITY_RISK_MEMO.md`](../robust_usage/OPPORTUNITY_RISK_MEMO.md) | History-indexed conditional rows and finite-trace TV already have substantial mathematical precedent. The robust check makes a useful finite contract concrete and handles different prefix masses. | The uncertainty sets are caller-declared, rectangular row polytopes / balls, not statistically estimated from API calls. Calibration would need a separate sampling design, confidence method, handling of nonstationarity/hidden state, and coverage argument. Measurements do not prove future rows fall inside the asserted sets. No provider-specific robust guarantee is currently established. |
| Source-to-normalized-IR finite-prefix refinement | Compiler correctness, simulation, and product-program literature; the project itself acknowledges this boundary in [`proof/RESULT.md`](../proof/RESULT.md) | Returned-state commit behavior, snapshot reads, request history, routes, and finite cycle prefixes are mechanized for the normalized representation. | Static verification of an IR does not prove source extraction or lowering. No claim of a source-to-IR compiler-correctness theorem is supported: paired descriptors and source/register names remain a trusted bridge. New agent workflow model checkers below also verify extracted graphs/plans under their abstractions, but that does not close OrchLang's bridge either. |
| Scoped execution of public source slices with replayed finite rational traces | Dynamic/differential testing, bounded model checking, and trace assurance (including [MAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18096v1)) | Actual bounded workflow code executes, ordered traces are captured, lossy projections receive negative controls, and an independent verifier checks the exported finite law. | The schedule is finite and hand-declared, the oracle is synthetic, and the environment is mocked/sandboxed. This is concrete capsule evidence about two source/runtime slices, not an API conformance study, a provider probability estimate, or evidence for a stochastic row-TV radius. |

## Closest new work by family

### Unary resource bounds and budgets

[Lamina's cost note](https://loom.technology/research/bounded-llm-cost/) is especially close to LLM token-resource claims. The author-hosted note states a decidable analyzer for a restricted Petri-net/workflow fragment, with a token-cost corollary under declared per-firing increments and sequential firing, and reports an author-run Cerebras evaluation over a parameter grid. It overlaps direct static token ceilings. Its target is a **unary cumulative upper bound**, not secret-conditioned equality of two usage laws. The empirical report belongs to that note; it is not independent validation of other workflows or providers.

[typed-resources v4](https://github.com/hernaninverso/typed-resources/tree/9ef2ee9bf9d97d6b125963a7dee9de2703f93f00) provides a Lean-checked sequential workflow cost calculus under declared call caps; [Costwright](https://github.com/eleata/costwright/tree/3d5212c56455ff9239c1d8d598b7b0317ee02674) is the associated AST-analysis software family. These are strong overlap for budget analysis and formal checking, with their own provider-cap assumptions and restricted concurrency/retry scope. The checked typed-resources record is a preprint/software repository, not a publisher paper. The Costwright tag is software, not peer review.

“Agent Contracts” must be disambiguated. [Ye and Tan's arXiv preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08815v1) proposes resource-bounded autonomous-agent contracts, runtime monitoring and budget/resource dimensions; the separate [`flyersworder/agent-contracts`](https://github.com/flyersworder/agent-contracts/tree/807a286434d7ff80ffb9c5495a806235f35cf9fb) repository contains its own software and whitepaper. They are not the same artifact as [ABC](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22302v1), “Agent Behavioral Contracts,” which studies runtime behavioral compliance/drift and compositional behavior contracts. The sources overlap contract and budget vocabulary; they do not by that fact establish exact ordered usage-law equivalence.

### Probabilistic workflow semantics and usage measurement

[PPDL](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/mandel26a.html) is a peer-reviewed ICML 2026/PMLR paper on probabilistic programming for LLM-based flows, uncertainty propagation, and inference-scaling experiments. It is important prior art against any broad claim that probabilistic multi-call workflows or their traces are unstudied. Its stated goal is not the same as equality of a selected per-call usage observer across secret assignments.

[ProMCP](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1967/) is a Findings of ACL 2026 publication that decomposes MCP token/latency cost across the host-client-server pipeline and reports measurements over a tool/server corpus. It establishes adjacent measurement/instrumentation work and highlights why serialized context/schema overhead matters. Its profiler does not prove relational confidentiality. In particular, token counters or framework `.content` fields cannot be treated as complete provider usage receipts without evidence from the actual provider boundary.

### Trace assurance and static workflow checks

[MAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.18096v1) defines Message-Action Traces with step/trace contracts, replay, localization, bounded adversarial perturbation search, fault injection, and governance. Its arXiv v1 says its focus is assurance methodology and that the full empirical study is ongoing. ENASE has a 2026 proceedings record for the work ([publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.5220/0014840300004015)); the accessible detailed text inspected here is the arXiv v1. MAT is the clearest recent neighbor for trace contracts and replay, but its reported contracts concern general workflow safety/reliability/governance rather than OrchLang's exact ordered modeled-usage law.

[Agentproof](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20356v1) extracts workflow graphs from several agent frameworks and checks structural properties and temporal policies. [CaMeLoT](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18674v1) translates a restricted generated CaMeL plan to a finite-state model for temporal verification. Both are recent preprints and important evidence that static workflow verification is already an active area. Their monitored properties and extraction boundaries differ from exact request/usage-trace equality and they do not prove OrchLang's AST/lowering bridge.

[Guardians of the Agents](https://doi.org/10.1145/3777544) is Erik Meijer's January 2026 Communications of the ACM article on formal verification of AI workflows. The [companion repository](https://github.com/metareflection/guardians/tree/59e52d9f1cbefdb344245304e260e647489a647b) describes itself as an implementation of those ideas. The publisher's article record is accessible as metadata, while its full text was not accessible in this check; therefore detailed claims here are limited to the publisher title/citation and the repository's own description. This is evidence of an adjacent publisher article and software project, not a basis to claim an identical verification property or to infer the review process.

### Information flow and agent privacy

[APPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24625v2) and [FlowSeal](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.14003v1) are recent preprints on information-flow enforcement/privacy leakage in tool-using agents. They make a broad “agent confidentiality is unaddressed” claim untenable. Their primary framing is control over sensitive content, provenance, tool access, and permitted release. That is closely related motivation but does not imply the same observation or theorem as exact equality / bounded TV of ordered usage events. **This is a scope distinction, not a claim that the systems cannot influence token counts.**

ABC additionally overlaps runtime behavioral contracts. It should not be dismissed because its title uses “contracts”; its stated target is a different compliance property. These papers also reinforce the need to state exactly which observer is protected: content, actions, tool calls, trace status, token counts, latency, and invoice data are distinct channels.

## Consolidated assessment and gaps

The strongest, defensible OrchLang positioning is a **bounded integration**: a restricted workflow compiler's declared request-observer relation and ordered call alignment are combined with a finite exact usage-law checker, a caller-parameterized robust full-history TV checker, a normalized-IR refinement theorem, and two scoped public-source execution capsules. Each component has a different premise and evidence class. The framework combines established relational-cost / information-flow / probabilistic-automata / coupling / compiler-refinement methods for this specific usage observer; it should not be described as inventing those general methods.

No checked primary source in this focused set was found to make this exact combination of claims for OrchLang's abstraction. That observation is not a priority result, and the search may have missed relevant work. More importantly, the present evidence does not resolve several substantial technical gaps:

* **API identity:** the compiler's descriptor is not a provider-complete serialized request, and the Coq result does not prove the AST extractor/lowering. Source-derived envelope measurements on one transport would bind only that exercised boundary. Original workflow API fidelity is unvalidated until its real provider, SDK/version, configuration, and serializer are reproduced.
* **Provider law:** no real provider/model usage receipt has been captured for the source capsules here, and the rational kernels are supplied inputs. Neither the exact-law checker nor offline capsules establish a real conditional usage distribution.
* **Uncertainty calibration:** row radii are not fitted. Even after an authorized sample, future coverage requires a separate statistical and operational argument; four public prompts under two profiles would be a small descriptive calibration sample only. It would not validate original models/workflows, establish probability-radius premises, or support stochastic row-TV claims by itself.
* **Observer coverage:** full usage receipt semantics, cached-input counters, reasoning-token counters, errors, stop status, retries, rate-limit charges, SDK-added prompt components, provider changes, and invoices are provider-specific. A wrapper's content string is not a receipt.
* **Evaluation breadth:** two capsules with authored finite mock supports are not a representative workflow corpus. Lamina's author-reported live experiment is directly relevant cost-bound prior art, but does not generalize to OrchLang or serve as its replication.

## Recommended novelty paragraph

> OrchLang is a restricted workflow-analysis prototype that checks a declared relational usage observer over secret-controlled alternatives. Its compiler aligns resolved model-contract identities and abstract DSL request descriptors; separate finite-state tools decide ordered usage-law equality or bound full-history trace distance under explicit rational-kernel or row-uncertainty contracts. A Coq artifact proves finite-prefix refinement for a normalized request-graph representation under stated bridge assumptions, and two source-slice capsules exercise bounded public LangGraph paths with finite mock oracles. These are application and artifact contributions built from established relational, automata, coupling, and refinement techniques. They do not establish provider-complete request equality, real-provider law calibration, a probability-radius premise, general source-to-IR correctness, or priority over all related work.
